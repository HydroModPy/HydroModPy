# Numerical tolerances used in HydroModPy tests

This document justifies every numerical tolerance used across the test
suite. Tolerances without a rationale here are considered a defect of
this document and should be tightened or justified in a follow-up PR.

## Principle

Each tolerance comes from one of three sources:

1. **Richardson extrapolation** - discretization error bound as `h → 0`.
2. **Machine epsilon** - `10 · ε · ‖f‖` for analytical solutions
   evaluated without discretization.
3. **Reference literature** - value published in a peer-reviewed
   benchmark (USGS, MODFLOW documentation, textbooks listed below).

References frequently cited:

- MacDonald & Harbaugh 1996, USGS OFR 96-485 (MODFLOW reference run).
- Zheng & Wang 1999, MT3DMS v5 documentation.
- Anderson, Woessner & Hunt 2015, *Applied Groundwater Modeling*, 2nd ed.
- ASME V&V 20-2009 terminology (verification vs validation).

The table below records the 130 tolerances enforced today. Every tolerance
must carry a rationale before it is merged.

## Update policy

- Tightening a tolerance is always allowed when the underlying test
  still passes: record the change in this file in the same PR.
- Relaxing a tolerance **requires** a new rationale line.
- When a new benchmark is introduced, add its tolerance(s) here before
  merging the test.
- 2026-06: MODFLOW 6 adopted the Newton formulation by default
  (`mf6_newton=True`), the robust choice for unconfined catchment cells. The
  per-case MF6 analytical overrides that had been fit to the old standard
  formulation (sub-mm to cm) were re-derived to the case benchmark
  (`tolerances.toml`, shared with the NWT and Boussinesq backends) or the
  documented literature value. On the 2D circular islands (rows 37, 38) the
  bias had a known cause, found in October 2026: under Newton, MODFLOW 6 builds
  the horizontal conductance from the full thickness of the two cells times the
  upstream saturation, and the cell top followed the DEM, so the transmissivity
  was too low where the top steps down from one cell to the next. See the
  2026-10-12 entry below. The 1D cases (rows 36, 39) have a flat
  top and a uniform thickness, so this cause does not apply there; their
  former bias was the boundary offset of the 2026-10-09 entry below. Determinism is guaranteed by the pinned solver release
  (`DEFAULT_RELEASE = "23.0"`), so these margins stay stable.
- 2026-10-09: the 1D analytical references now pose their heads where the
  model holds them, at the centres of the boundary cells
  (`validation_cases/shared/strip_geometry.py`). Their thresholds were derived
  again as twice the measured error, one file shared by NWT and MF6. What is
  left is the first-order upstream weighting of the saturated thickness under
  Newton and UPW, checked by `test_grid_convergence_dupuit_1d.py` (rows 98, 99).
- 2026-10-09: the Brutsaert recession cases test what their names say. The
  thin case drains to the substratum and is read on the late, separable
  recession (rows 100 to 103); the deep case takes 100 steps per period and
  MODFLOW-NWT runs chi-MD with closures under the compared discharges (rows
  1, 73, 104 to 107). The former relaxations (0.23 on every solver, 1.0 on
  NWT) are gone.
- 2026-10-09: a twin calibration test records the threshold it asserts, a
  multiple of the case tolerance per method (`TwinCheck` in
  `tests/validation/calibration/helpers.py`); a distance it does not assert is
  recorded without a threshold. The flux-only noisy twin is a wiring smoke
  test: 6 evaluations per method, point methods within 8 times the tolerance.
- 2026-10-09: the Boussinesq solver is validated twice, through PETSc and
  through the default engine (`auto`: dense `local` up to 400 cells,
  `scipy_sparse` above), both solving each stress period in its sub-steps
  (rows 113 to 123). The validation runtime starts from a neutral state, never
  from the reference; the island and interception references and meshes were
  rebuilt; the patch test documents the two-point flux on skewed triangles.
- 2026-10-10: the drainage cases derive their drain conductance from K, so the
  leakance is the same on every mesh; the Voronoi islands hold the sea level on

## Cross-platform determinism

The tolerances above assume that tests run with:

- BLAS single-thread (`OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1`, `MKL_NUM_THREADS=1`,
  `OMP_NUM_THREADS=1`) - enforced by `tests/conftest.py`.
- Fixed RNG seed (42) - enforced autouse via `_deterministic_seeds`.
- No timestamps inside goldens (excluded from signature stats).

Deviating from those assumptions requires documenting the new
tolerance envelope alongside the failing test.
